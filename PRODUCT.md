# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Israeli youth ages 12–21, their parents, donors, and civil society partners. Primary audience is the youth themselves and parents researching programs. Secondary: institutional funders and civil organizations considering collaboration. Context: visiting on mobile or desktop, often after seeing the movement mentioned socially or via word-of-mouth, looking to understand whether this is the right place for themselves or their child.

## Product Purpose

Five Fingers (חמש אצבעות) is an Israeli educational-social movement. The website communicates the movement's philosophy and values, explains each program to prospective participants and parents, and converts visits into program registrations. Success: a visitor understands the movement's ethos deeply enough to want to join, and finds their path to registration.

## Brand Personality

Bold, gritty, human. Not an institution — a movement. The brand speaks like someone who has already entered the arena: direct, earned, no corporate distance. Three words: **disciplined, alive, Israeli**.

## Anti-references

Not corporate or generic youth-org websites. Not polished American nonprofit template sites. Not government ministry branding. Not Israeli tech startup aesthetic (navy-and-orange SaaS landing pages). Nothing that looks like it could belong to any other organization; everything must be specifically Five Fingers.

## Design Principles

1. **The arena before the arena** — Every design choice should feel like it was made by someone already in the arena, not someone describing it from outside. Urgency and physicality over description.
2. **Earned, not explained** — Show who we are through texture, imagery, and specificity. Don't announce values; embody them.
3. **Israeli, not international** — The experience should feel native to Israel: the language, the density, the directness, the scale of the people involved.
4. **Movement, not product** — This is not a service being sold. The design should create a sense of belonging before registration, not funnel visitors toward a conversion.
5. **Every word earns its place** — RTL Hebrew copy is already dense; each additional heading, eyebrow, and subtitle must justify its presence or be cut.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Hebrew RTL primary. Bilingual potential (Hebrew/English). Gender-neutral language required throughout (see CLAUDE.md gender-neutral policy). WCAG AA minimum for contrast. Reduced-motion support already implemented via GSAP conditional. Accessibility widget in place.
